## Think: Week 3 (January 25 - 31)

Last module we asked whether a chatbot *understands* us. This module asks a more practical question: can we trust what it tells us?

Ted Chiang's "ChatGPT Is a Blurry JPEG of the Web" offers one of the most useful metaphors for how large language models work. Chiang compares a model to a heavily compressed image: it keeps the overall shape of what it was trained on but loses the fine detail, and when it fills in the missing detail, the result *looks* right without necessarily *being* right. Matthew Kirschenbaum's "Prepare for the Textpocalypse" (by the same author as last module's DH introduction) imagines what happens when the internet fills up with machine-generated writing, and when future models are trained on that writing in turn. Read both with this module's exercise in mind: if a model is a blurry copy of the web, where should we expect it to be blurriest?

**To do this week:**

- Read [Chiang](https://www.newyorker.com/tech/annals-of-technology/chatgpt-is-a-blurry-jpeg-of-the-web) and [Kirschenbaum](https://www.theatlantic.com/technology/archive/2023/03/ai-chatgpt-writing-language-models/673318/). (Paywall? Both are available free through the UCF Library databases.)
- Watch the lecture video in Webcourses and look through the [Module Two slides](slides/moduletwo.html).
- Start thinking about a topic you know well enough to fact-check (see Step 1 below).

### Exercise: Fact-Checking the Machine

Fact-checking and source evaluation are core humanities skills, and they matter more than ever when a chatbot can produce a confident, well-written answer in seconds. In this exercise, you'll ask an AI assistant to explain a topic and give you sources, and then you'll check its work.

#### Step 1: Choose a Topic You Can Check

Pick a topic from literature, history, art, or culture that you already know something about, so you'll be able to spot mistakes. Some ideas:

- A novel, poem, film, or game you've studied closely
- A historical event, person, or place connected to your hometown or to Florida
- A musician, artist, or cultural movement you follow
- A topic from another class you're taking this semester

Narrower is better: "the Harlem Renaissance" is a big topic, but "Zora Neale Hurston's years in Eatonville, Florida" is one you can actually verify.

#### Step 2: Ask Without Search

In a new chat, with web search turned **off** if your tool allows it (look for a search or globe icon near the text box), paste in this prompt with your topic filled in:

> Write a 300-word overview of [your topic] for a college student. Then list five scholarly sources (books or journal articles) I could read to learn more, with full citations.

**Screenshot 1:** the response, including the list of sources.

#### Step 3: Check Every Source

Check each of the five sources using the [UCF Library search](https://library.ucf.edu/) and [Google Scholar](https://scholar.google.com/). Sort each one into a category:

- **Real and accurate:** the source exists, the citation details are correct, and it's actually about what the chatbot says it's about
- **Real but wrong:** the source exists, but some details are off (wrong year, wrong author, wrong journal) or it isn't really about your topic
- **Fabricated:** you can't find any evidence the source exists

Then read the 300-word overview closely and mark anything you know (or can confirm) is wrong, oversimplified, or missing.

#### Step 4: Ask Again, With Search

Start a new chat, turn web search **on**, and paste in the same prompt. (If your tool always searches, use a second tool for Step 2 instead.) Check this second set of sources the same way. **Screenshot 2:** this response with its sources. Notice what *kinds* of sources each version prefers: scholarly books and articles, encyclopedia entries, news, or company websites and blogs?

#### Step 5: Make a Results Table

Make a simple table of all ten sources with three columns: the source, its category (real and accurate, real but wrong, or fabricated), and a short note on what you found. You can type it into your post or share a screenshot of it.
